# Research Article acceptance contract

Current editorial direction supersedes prior length targets. Oxford Academic Research Article guidance is typically 5,000 words and 4–8 displays (https://academic.oup.com/neo/pages/author-guidelines). Aim for about 3,000 narrative words where justified, with scope to extend towards 4,000 for substantive analysis. There is no minimum project length.

ScholarOne → Neonatology → Instructions & Forms → Submission guidelines pointed to https://karger.com/neo/pages/guidelines on 3 November 2026. The journal describes these quantities as guidance. Our editorial targets are 230–250 words in a four-part abstract, main text approaching 2,500 words (count Introduction through Conclusion including headings, report table material separately), about 25 relevant references in first-citation order, and six numbered displays with in-text callouts. Use space for clinical interpretation, reproducibility and limitations. Never invent data or pad citations to reach a target.

Baseline assessment of the author's saved Word draft: 195 abstract words, 1,531 narrative words, four clinical papers, two tables and no figures. Retain both tables. Explain observation, delayed escalation, observational confounding, financing and the distinction between population prediction and individual management. Show pathway structure, deterministic transfers, paired simulation uncertainty and sensitivity to effect transportability. Anchor additional references to the specific data or methods they support.

Acceptance requires figure values from frozen files, intact original numerical claims, consistency between Word and Markdown, genuine Times New Roman heading styles (H1 14 bold, H2 12 bold, H3 12 italic), preserved author edits, and separate reporting of unresolved declarations, four independent reviews and fiscal evidence.

## Scope, decision and reporting acceptance

Use the [original executive briefing](../../../outputs/Panda_Vision_economic_evaluation_briefing_note.docx) to identify the team's questions, the [agreed product scope](../../product.md) to interpret later refinements, and the [clinical manuscript](../../../docs/manuscript/neonatology-manuscript.md), [supplement](../../../docs/manuscript/neonatology-supplement.md) and [CHEERS map](../../../docs/manuscript/neonatology-cheers.md) as reporting evidence. The contract distinguishes a question answered by the model from an observation that would require local data.

Simulated feedback cannot fulfil any independent review. Do not change frozen estimates to close a reporting gap.

## Current publisher reconciliation — 3 November 2026

The Oxford Academic author-guidelines page and its linked Research Article Word
template are the current format sources. The page uses a typical 5,000 words and
4–8 combined displays. The template specifies Introduction, Methods, Results and
Conclusion abstract labels and 3–5 keywords. A 250-word abstract ceiling is our
editorial choice, not a text-abstract limit established by the public page.
Use about 3,000–4,000 substantive narrative words without padding. Earlier 2,500-word
and 230-word minimum targets above are historical and superseded.

Use Vancouver citations in parentheses, editable tables at the end and embedded
figures in numerical order. Provide separate funding, conflict, ethics and data
availability statements, author roles and AI disclosure. Add the encouraged lay
summary and keep the graphical abstract separate. A secondary descriptive
administrative-data component needs an applicable STROBE/RECORD map alongside
CHEERS and CHEERS-VOI; no checklist supplies missing approval or causal validity.

This lane prepares a private candidate. Its numerical source is the sealed
project-wide information revision, with frozen default claims preserved. Patient
extracts and derived documents remain outside all repositories. The article
checkout's author edit remains untouched. Editorial implementation archival is
separate from the existing submission acceptance conditions, which remain open.

# Neonatology Research Article publication contract

Current editorial direction supersedes prior length targets. Oxford Academic Research Article guidance is typically 5,000 words and 4–8 displays (https://academic.oup.com/neo/pages/author-guidelines). Aim for about 3,000 narrative words where justified, with scope to extend towards 4,000 for substantive analysis. There is no minimum project length.

Score the manuscript and supplement independently, each out of 10,000. This is a local editorial decision aid. CHEERS measures reporting coverage across the packet. Keep its item-level findings alongside the document scores. A manuscript may earn full credit for clear headline reporting with a precise supplement pointer; a supplement earns it by supplying the underlying assumptions, data or method. Never require author declarations to be repeated in the supplement.

Each of the following ten domains carries 1,000 points. Use the same anchors for both documents: 0 absent or materially misleading; 250 mentioned without enough information to interpret; 500 interpretable but a material requirement is missing; 750 substantially met with a bounded correction or verification outstanding; 1,000 all stated criteria evidenced for that document’s role. No interpolation. A strict score above 9,950 requires full credit in every domain at this resolution. Record the exact files, evidence and reason for every deduction. An independent reviewer should repeat the assessment after material revisions.

| Domain | Evidence required for full credit |
|---|---|
| Journal and article fit | Original neonatal research question, appropriate Research Article structure and displays, verified against the active publisher instructions. Supplement is clearly navigable and corresponds to the article. |
| Team decision | Population, pilot and expansion comparators, investment question, resource and budget horizons traceable to the team brief. |
| Clinical interpretation | Separate observed and modelled endpoints, immediate transfer and later rescue, ascertainment window and implications for safe care. |
| Evidence and bias | Trace key inputs to evidence or labelled assumptions; explain selection, confounding and local transportability. |
| Economic reporting | Applicable CHEERS items located; perspectives, resource versus cash accounting, price years, conversion and discounting intelligible. |
| Information reporting | Applicable CHEERS-VOI core elaborations and S1–S6 located; name decision, information, population, horizon, methods, costs and decision consequences. |
| Analytical validity | Matched comparators, plausible distributions, converged estimates, uncertainty and sensitivity sufficient for the conclusions. |
| Reproducibility | Versioned sources, settings, outputs and verification for every headline claim, including new analyses. |
| Communication | Main findings interpretable for neonatal readers; figures, captions, units, accessibility and cross-references support the document’s role. |
| Transparency and completeness | Disclose funding, conflicts, engagement and ethics status in the appropriate document; expose open fiscal and research assumptions and decisions requiring author input. |

Submission acceptance requires both scores above 9,950, a completed packet-level checklist, author declarations, verified computational and document checks, and actual returns from the four independent reviewers. An open fiscal question may be published as explicitly conditional only. No score establishes payer liability.

Reporting selection: use [CHEERS 2022](https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/good-practices/cheers) with [CHEERS-VOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414276/). [Karger](https://karger.com/neo/pages/guidelines) requires the appropriate STROBE checklist when reporting observational findings. This paper models policies using aggregate inputs and published evidence. A future comparative registry reporting participant outcomes should apply [STROBE cohort](https://www.strobe-statement.org/); add [RECORD](https://www.record-statement.org/) when using routinely collected health data, including linked administrative outcomes. Prospective purpose-built registry collection alone does not trigger RECORD. CONSORT applies if allocation is randomised, PRISMA if a systematic review is reported and TRIPOD if an individual prediction model is developed or validated. None describes the present primary analysis.

Journal source discrepancy: the current public Karger Research Article page describes a typical 5,000 words and 4–8 displays. The earlier portal record in spec.md used 2,500 and 4–6. Retain 2,500 as the team's concise writing target and recheck the live portal instructions before upload. Neither typical word count is a minimum.

## Current interpretation

The current Oxford page and linked template supersede the historical source-
discrepancy paragraph above: 5,000 words and 4–8 displays are typical guidance,
not a minimum. The project aims for substantive 3,000–4,000-word narrative text.
A 250-word text-abstract ceiling is an editorial target; the public page's explicit
250-word statement concerns its encouraged lay summary. The linked template
supplies the four abstract headings and 3–5 keyword recommendation.

Do not award full credit merely to exceed a desired score. Document roles and
explicit assumptions matter: local evidence that remains unknown is not invented.
Submission acceptance still requires the complete declaration and independent
review conditions stated above. Editorial preparation may be archived separately
only with its actual verification and remaining acceptance clearly recorded.

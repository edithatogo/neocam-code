# Neonatal teleconsultation evidence appraisal

Reviewed 2 November 2026 from four user-supplied publisher PDFs. Page locators below count PDF pages from one. Source IDs and DOIs match the full-text verification register. The accompanying file hashes identify the exact copies reviewed.

**Haynes 2020 (HAY2020).** Six rural California hospitals referred birth-encounter consultations to UC Davis between August 2014 and June 2018. Multiple telephone calls could belong to one encounter. An encounter involving both modes counted as video. Infants already discharged after birth were excluded. The authors identified 317 encounters and reported five charts unavailable. The endpoint was transfer to higher-level care **within 24 hours of consultation**. Video and telephone groups had transfer rates of 64% and 82%. The adjusted odds ratio was 0.48 (95% CI 0.26–0.90), accounting for site, gestation, birthweight, five-minute Apgar and TRIPS-II severity. Clinicians chose the consultation mode. Residual selection bias and the absence of adjudication of transfer necessity limit causal interpretation. The study supplies no estimate of later rescue, safety of retention, cost or device equivalence. Neocam’s eventual-transfer reference mapping therefore requires an explicit extrapolation. Reviewers should compare its immediate and eventual endpoint scenarios. [Methods and Table 2, PDF pp. 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acap.2020.02.013)

**Armfield 2012 (ARM2012).** Queensland observational service evaluation covering one tertiary centre and four peripheral nurseries over 12 months from December 2008. Clinicians selected video or usual telephone care. Independent expert review of recordings and case histories judged five of 19 video encounters to have avoided retrieval, including four helicopter trips. These counterfactual judgments carry uncertainty. The health-service analysis in 2009 AUD included annualised equipment, maintenance, communications, implementation staffing, retrieval staffing and transport, plus the estimated difference between tertiary and peripheral nursery costs. Software development was excluded. Nursery valuation used casemix funding, inferred DRGs and assumed stays. The authors estimated AUD 54,400 net savings for the study service and projected AUD 206,073 under expansion to all retrieval consultations. Neither amount establishes current marginal savings or released cash. [Methods and Tables 1–7, PDF pp. 2–5](https://doi.org/10.1258/jtt.2012.gth101)

**Yoo 2022 (YOO2022).** California payer decision model comparing video availability with telephone-only care per live birth in level I nurseries. Its primary consultation measures come from the same UC Davis six-hospital network and August 2014–June 2018 period as Haynes. Patient-level overlap cannot be quantified from these reports. Treat the estimates as dependent. In 2017 USD, Table I uses $5,344 annual operating cost per nursery, $1,467 per ground transfer and $21,327 for either air mode. Treatment costs use administrative proxies, including $16,745 for tertiary care and $3,033 for a consulted infant staying locally. The probabilistic benefit-to-cost ratio averaged 2.23 (95% interval 0.7–6.0), with savings in 75% of simulations. Table IV on the supplied electronic page 63.e1 includes break-even analysis. Table IV gives six users per nursery per year, while Table I prints three as its point estimate. Resolve this discrepancy before reproducing unit operating costs. Transferability depends on consultation uptake, utilisation, case mix and local transport costs. [Methods and Tables I–IV, PDF pp. 2–7](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpeds.2021.11.076)

**Thao 2022 (THAO2022).** US health-system decision tree for infants requiring consultation after delivery at a non-NICU hospital. The perspective includes both hospitals and transport, through the first hospitalisation. The model explicitly takes its 82% baseline transfer probability and adjusted odds ratio of 0.48 from Haynes. Its 2020 USD estimates use a $1,821 consultation cost borrowed from paediatric critical care and stroke programmes, Medicare transport formulas with a 200-mile round trip and equal air/ground shares, and hospital discharge cost proxies ($28,615 NICU and $3,067 non-NICU). Normal newborn DRG 795 was excluded from the hospital costing. The reported $11,168 saving per consultation is a model result. A reconstruction using the rounded table inputs reproduces the reported video cost approximately if 0.82 is multiplied by 0.48 (transfer probability 0.394). Converting the stated odds ratio to a probability instead gives 0.686. This is a discrepancy inferred from the published numbers; the implementation was unavailable. Do not transfer the saving or treat it as a second effect estimate. [Methods, Tables 1–2 and results, PDF pp. 2–5](https://doi.org/10.1089/tmj.2021.0527)
